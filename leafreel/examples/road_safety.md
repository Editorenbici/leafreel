# Walking Safely to School

Every day millions of children walk to school, and a few simple habits make the trip much safer. The first habit is to know your route. Before you leave, look at a map of your neighborhood and choose the path with sidewalks, crosswalks and traffic lights. Walk it once with an adult so you know where it turns and where it is busy.

Traffic signs and signals are a language that everybody shares. A red traffic light means stop, and a green light means you may cross, but only after you look left and right and make sure the cars have really stopped. A zebra crossing is a set of white stripes painted on the road to show where people cross. A stop sign is red with eight sides, and it tells drivers to come to a full stop.

When there is no sidewalk, walk on the left side facing the traffic so you can see the cars coming. Walk in a line, never side by side, and stay as far from the road as you can. Wear bright colors, or a reflective band, when it is early in the morning or getting dark, because drivers see bright colors from farther away.

Never run into the street to get a ball or to catch a bus. Wait until the bus has stopped completely and the door opens. Keep your phone in your bag while you walk and while you cross, because your eyes and ears are your best safety tools. If you ever feel lost or unsafe, stop, find a trusted adult such as a teacher, a shopkeeper or a police officer, and ask for help.

Groups can make the route safer too. A walking bus is a group of children who walk to school together with one or two adults, picking up friends at agreed stops along the way. Neighbors can ask the city for a speed bump, a new crosswalk or a better streetlight at a dangerous corner.

Project idea: draw your own route to school, mark the crosswalks and traffic lights, and write three safety rules for your friends.
